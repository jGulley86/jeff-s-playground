## 1. Why This Search Exists

We currently outsource fabrication of core metal components and have made the strategic decision to bring it in-house. The business case rests on three things: compressing engineering iteration loops from weeks to days, reducing cost per unit below outside-supplier pricing, and controlling quality and supply continuity as volumes scale. This hire is the linchpin of that decision — the role builds the department from a blank floor, then runs it.

This is a true build-and-run mandate with capital to deploy. The successful candidate will report directly to the COO and sit at the company operations-strategy table.

## 2. The Role in One Paragraph

A VP of Fabrication with deep metals manufacturing experience who will select and commission equipment (fiber laser cutting, press brakes, CNC machining, turret punching, welding, bending, finishing), design the floor for flow, hire the crew from zero, stand up the quality system, own the make-vs-buy transition sequence, and run the operation on Theory of Constraints principles with full P&L-style accountability for cost per unit, lead time, and on-time delivery. Full details in the attached job description.

## 3. The Candidate Profile — Who Wins This Role

### Backgrounds that fit (in rough order of preference)
1. **GM / VP / Director of Operations at a precision sheet-metal job shop or contract metal fabricator** — they live in the exact equipment set, quote work daily (so they understand cost per part cold), and are used to high-mix, fast-turn environments. Best fit if they've also done an equipment expansion or greenfield/brownfield buildout.
2. **Operations leader who built or scaled a captive fab shop inside an OEM** — robotics, automation, material handling, agricultural/construction equipment, HVAC, appliance, or trailer/truck-body manufacturing. They've already won the make-vs-buy argument once.
3. **Plant manager at a metals-intensive manufacturer stepping up**, if they have demonstrable buildout experience and the commercial/financial range for a VP seat.

### Backgrounds that look right but usually aren't
- Pure assembly or electronics operations leaders with no metals depth.
- Big-company VPs who managed fabrication through three layers of staff and haven't touched equipment selection, hiring, or layout in a decade.
- Consultants or corporate continuous-improvement leaders without line ownership of a P&L and a floor.

### Geography
Atlanta metro and the broader Southeast manufacturing belt (Georgia, Tennessee, the Carolinas, Alabama) should be the first hunting ground — strong fabrication talent pool, easier close, no relocation friction. National search acceptable for an exceptional fit; role is five-days-on-site in Norcross, so the candidate must relocate, not commute remotely.

## 4. Non-Negotiables (screen out without these)

1. **10+ years in metals fabrication/manufacturing, 5+ leading multi-discipline fab operations.**
2. **Hands-on equipment fluency** — fiber laser, press brakes, CNC mills/lathes, welding, punching, bending, finishing. Test: they can discuss tonnage, tooling, nesting efficiency, and weld fixturing specifics without hand-waving.
3. **Has stood up or significantly expanded a fabrication operation** — equipment justification through commissioning and ramp. Ask for the capex number, the timeline, and what went wrong.
4. **Theory of Constraints fluency, applied — not just read.** See screening probes below. This is a genuine requirement from the executive team, not a nice-to-have.
5. **Financial acumen** — can build and defend a make-vs-buy model and a capex payback case to a CFO and board.
6. **Startup pace tolerance** — evidence of operating with ambiguity, thin staff, and weekly (not quarterly) decision cycles.

## 5. Screening Probes (use in the firm's first-round screens)

**Theory of Constraints — the key differentiator. Probe hard:**
- "Walk me through a time you identified and elevated a constraint. What was the constraint, how did you exploit it before spending money, and what did throughput do?" *(Strong answers follow the five focusing steps in order — exploit before elevate. Weak answers jump straight to buying a machine.)*
- "When is a labor-efficiency improvement bad for the business?" *(Strong answers: when it builds inventory at a non-constraint or starves/blocks the bottleneck; they should reference throughput vs. cost-world thinking.)*
- "How would you schedule a high-mix fab shop?" *(Listen for drum-buffer-rope or constraint-paced scheduling, not just 'we used our ERP's MRP run.')*

**Build experience:**
- "You have an empty 40,000 sq ft floor and a parts list. Walk me through your first 90 days." *(Strong answers start with demand/part-family analysis and make-vs-buy sequencing before equipment shopping.)*
- "Tell me about an equipment purchase you got wrong."

**Financial:**
- "Take a part you moved in-house. Walk me through the cost model — what did you include, and where did actuals diverge?"

**Startup fit:**
- "What's the smallest team you've led day-to-day, and how recently have you personally set up or run a machine?"

## 6. Red Flags

- Can name TOC concepts but every example is from a book or a course, not their floor.
- Every accomplishment is "we" with no specific personal decision they can defend.
- Has only ever run mature operations with established processes and full support staff.
- Talks about utilization of every machine as a goal (cost-world thinking — the opposite of what we want).
- Unwilling to be on the floor daily, or probes early about remote/hybrid flexibility.
- Job-hops through ops leadership roles at under two years each with no buildout to show for it.

## 7. Assessment Process (proposed — confirm with COO)

1. **Search firm screen** — résumé plus the probes above; submit a written candidate brief per finalist.
2. **COO interview** — mandate, pace, and mutual fit.
3. **Floor-and-case round (on-site, Norcross):** facility walk, then a working session — we hand the candidate a representative part drawing and rough volumes; they sketch the equipment set, layout logic, staffing plan, and cost model live. This is the decisive round; we're hiring how they think.
4. **Executive panel** — CEO, CFO (financial-case deep dive), head of engineering (DFM partnership fit).
5. **References** — must include one person who worked *for* them on a shop floor and one executive they defended a capex case to.

## 9. Selling the Role — Talking Points for Sourcing Calls

- **Builder's mandate:** design the floor, pick the equipment, hire the crew — from zero. Almost no VP role offers this; most inherit someone else's decisions.
- **Speed:** decisions that take a quarter elsewhere take a week here.
- **Visibility:** their work shows up in the product and the P&L within months, and they report to the COO directly — no layers.
- **A leadership team that already speaks their language:** the executive team runs on Theory of Constraints; they won't spend year one explaining why the bottleneck matters.
- **Growth-stage robotics in metro Atlanta** — equity upside plus a deep regional manufacturing talent pool to recruit from.
