# 01 — Where We Are (June 2026)

## What H1 delivered
Tray — the new product — launched. That was the half's defining commitment and it shipped: the product exists, is in production, and is in front of customers. The established robot business kept serving its installed base, and two structural programs got underway: the field service system rollout in Rasheen's org and the factory consolidation under Eric.

## What H1 didn't deliver, and why
Tray sales. The diagnosis is go-to-market — pipeline and sales motion — which sits outside this org. But ops is not a bystander: Tray's bill of materials costs roughly twice what it must for the company to price Tray to win and still hold the margin path to cash-flow-positive. Today, go-to-market masks the cost problem. The moment selling improves, cost becomes the stall — and fixing it then means re-engineering under load. H2 exists to make sure that never happens.

## The binding constraint
**Tray unit cost.** Defended in three sentences:

The true system constraint is the market — demand is soft — and ops cannot fix go-to-market directly. The strongest operational lever on demand is price, and Tray's bill of materials must fall by half before the company can price to win without abandoning the margin path. Everything else ops does — throughput, facilities, systems, service — has slack while demand is soft, so any H2 effort that neither cuts Tray cost nor protects customer trust is subordinate by definition.

Customer trust earns the second clause because in Robotics-as-a-Service, references are the sales asset: a cheap robot that fails at a marquee customer site sets the company back further than the cost program advances it.

## Inherited commitments
1. **Field service system rollout** (Rasheen, mid-flight). Honored — and subordinated: its H2 justification is cost-to-serve and service quality at the installed base, not feature completeness. Scope is frozen at what achieves that.
2. **Factory move/consolidation** (Eric, in motion). Honored — and redirected: with demand soft, consolidation is the right move only if the consolidated factory is designed around the lower-cost Tray build, not a relocation of today's process. One disruption, not two.
3. **A performance situation in a critical seat.** Acknowledged here, handled privately, resolved early in H2 — an underpowered seat anywhere near the cost program is a constraint multiplier.

## Operating environment
Cash is comfortable and the posture is growth-first, which for this half means: fund the cost program and customer trust without hesitation, and decline spend that merely adds capacity ahead of demand that hasn't arrived. The 30-month roadmap (IPO-ready, cash-flow-positive) makes H2 cost work compound — every dollar out of Tray's bill of materials recurs with every unit the recovery eventually sells.
