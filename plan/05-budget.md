# 05 — Budget

Directional by design. Numbers appear only where a decision hangs on them. Gaby owns translating this into the operating budget; deviations route through the gates below, not through hallway approvals.

## Opex envelope by function (direction, July → December)
- **Manufacturing (Eric):** flat on the existing factory — direct labor frozen — plus the fabrication team as the named, gated exception.
- **Sourcing (Anjana):** up slightly — the cost engineer. The single highest-leverage opex dollar in the plan.
- **Service Delivery (Rasheen):** flat, with cost-to-serve falling as legacy tools retire in October and the field service system takes hold.
- **Purchasing (Lan):** flat, absorbing transactional sourcing within current headcount.
- **Quality (Marques):** flat — capacity comes from the deleted calendar, not budget.
- **Finance (Gaby) and Operations Programs (Ada):** flat.
- **Tech Services:** to zero — the function is dissolved; its remaining run-rate funds most of the additions above.

**Net: up modestly, on purpose.** The increase is two constraint-serving additions, largely offset by the Tech Services deletion and the freezes. Growth-first cash posture pays for cost capability, not capacity.

## Capex — every dollar tied to a named initiative
- **Fabrication equipment (3B): under $1M.** Bias to proven used equipment — cheaper and, more importantly, faster than new lead times.
- **Consolidation fit-out (3C):** committed and already in motion; fab floor space included in the locked July layout so it isn't a second project.
- **Supplier tooling (1A):** gated part-by-part — tooling is approved only where the piece-price saving pays it back inside the roadmap window.
- **Orphan capex rule:** any capital request that can't name its initiative is cancelled by default. There is no "general" capex line in H2.

## The three largest commitments, each with a decision gate
1. **Fabrication equipment — under $1M.** Gate: 3A's make-versus-buy and payback case, August, signed by Jeff and Gaby. Long-lead orders release only after the case clears; if the case shrinks the part list, the equipment list shrinks with it.
2. **Tray long-lead inventory.** The easiest way to lose cash quietly in a soft-demand half. Gate: any buy beyond committed customer demand requires either a named customer signal or an explicit growth bet approved by Chris — monthly review with Gaby, and 1A's resourcing must not quietly obligate volume commitments that are inventory by another name.
3. **Field service system contract.** Gate: the July scope freeze sizes the contract; nothing is licensed for features cut from H2 scope. Expansion decisions belong to 2027 planning, not to the vendor's renewal calendar.

## Cost out of the budget (delete and simplify passes, banked)
- Tech Services run-rate — redeployed against the hires; net reduction after both.
- Legacy field tools retired in October — duplicate spend ends at the gate, not "eventually."
- Old-site costs end with the September consolidation — lease overlap past September is a risk trigger (07), not a budget line.
- Program reviews, deferred audits, and cancelled pre-plan requisitions — individually small, collectively the proof that flat envelopes are real.

## Standing challenge
In July, Gaby runs one scrub: every recurring line that neither serves one of the three objectives nor keeps the installed base running is cut or must be re-justified to Jeff in person. Spend that subordinates to nothing subordinates the plan to it.
