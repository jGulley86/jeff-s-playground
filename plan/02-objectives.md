# 02 — H2 2026 Objectives

Three objectives, not five. Each traces to the 30-month roadmap (IPO-ready, cash-flow-positive), has a December 31 definition of done, one accountable owner, and names its relationship to the binding constraint — Tray unit cost.

## Objective 1 — Cut Tray's bill of materials in half
**Owner: Anjana.**
**Constraint relationship:** elevates the constraint directly. This is the objective the half is organized around.
**Roadmap trace:** halved BOM is what makes Tray priceable to win while holding the gross-margin path to cash-flow-positive; every dollar removed recurs with every unit sold from here to IPO.
**Definition of done (Dec 31):** a released Tray bill of materials at half of today's cost — quotes or signed agreements covering the full bill, design changes released through Quality, and the first production-intent units built at the new cost. Paper savings don't count; built units do.

## Objective 2 — Make every customer site a reference
**Owner: Rasheen.**
**Constraint relationship:** subordinates to the constraint. The true system constraint is demand; while go-to-market rebuilds, ops' job is to ensure no prospect ever hears a bad reference. Trust is the one demand lever ops holds besides price.
**Roadmap trace:** revenue retention and reference-ability are what convert a go-to-market recovery into bookings — and churn at the installed base would invalidate the growth story before it starts.
**Definition of done (Dec 31):** every active customer account is referenceable — willing to take a prospect call or site visit; no account on a watch list for performance reasons; Tray's early units performing at parity with the established product in the eyes of their operators.

## Objective 3 — Stand up the Fabrication Organization
**Owner: Eric.**
**Constraint relationship:** elevates the constraint structurally. In-house fabrication attacks the make-versus-buy layer of Tray cost that negotiation and resourcing can't reach, and shortens supply lead times as a compounding benefit.
**Roadmap trace:** owned fabrication capability bends unit cost durably and de-risks supply ahead of the ramp the roadmap requires — margin structure an IPO story can stand on.
**Definition of done (Dec 31):** a fabrication shop operating inside the consolidated factory, producing an agreed set of Tray parts at or below their cost-out targets, with Marques's quality sign-off and a make-versus-buy decision recorded for every fabricated part. An org chart with no parts shipping is not done.

## Challenged and demoted
- **Factory consolidation** — selected against as an objective: it is a project in disguise. It survives as an initiative serving Objectives 1 and 3 (the consolidated factory is designed around the cheaper Tray build and houses fabrication — one disruption, not two).
- **Field service system rollout** — a project, not an objective. It becomes an initiative under Objective 2, scope frozen at what improves cost-to-serve and service quality.
- **IPO-readiness controls, ramp readiness, org strength** — offered, not selected. They appear in this plan only where they subordinate to the three objectives above; none earns its own banner this half.
