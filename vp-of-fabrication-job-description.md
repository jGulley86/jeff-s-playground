# Vice President of Fabrication

**Location:** Norcross, GA (on-site)
**Reports to:** Chief Operations Officer
**Type:** Full-time, Executive
**Keywords:** Metal Fabrication, Sheet Metal, VP Manufacturing, Fabrication Operations, Theory of Constraints

---

## About Us

We're a venture-backed robotics company in metro Atlanta, growing fast and building hardware that has to ship on time, every time. Fabricated metal is the backbone of our product — and we've decided that owning fabrication, rather than outsourcing it, is how we win on speed, cost, and quality.

## The Mission

Build our in-house fabrication operation from a blank floor to a production engine. You will select the equipment, design the layout, hire the team, define the processes, and then run the department you created — with real capital to deploy and real accountability for what it returns.

The goal is simple to state and hard to do: fabricated parts in days instead of weeks, at a cost per unit that beats our outside suppliers, at a quality level engineering can trust without re-checking.

## How We Operate

We run at startup speed: we ship, measure, and iterate. Decisions that take a quarter elsewhere take a week here. We expect an aggressive drive for action — a strong plan executed today beats a perfect plan executed next month. You'll have the autonomy that pace requires; we expect the judgment it demands.

On the floor, we think in terms of Eli Goldratt's **Theory of Constraints** — not as trivia, but as a daily operating system: identify the constraint, exploit it, subordinate everything else to it, elevate it, repeat. We measure the operation in throughput, inventory, and operating expense, and we expect you to know why a "cost savings" that starves the bottleneck is a loss. If drum-buffer-rope scheduling and throughput accounting feel like home, you'll fit right in.

## What You'll Own

You will carry full authority over the fabrication department: its capital budget, its headcount plan, its layout, and its metrics.

### Build Phase (first 6–12 months)
- **Stand up the department:** equipment selection, procurement, facility layout, installation, and commissioning — designed around flow, not departmental silos.
- **Sequence the make-vs-buy transition:** model which part families move in-house first and prove out the economics of each transition before committing the next.
- **Hire the team:** recruit operators, fabricators, welders, and supervisors; build a training pipeline and a culture of ownership. Expect to grow the team from zero to a full production crew on your watch.
- **Define the quality system:** first-article inspection, in-process checks, incoming material verification, and the documentation to support it — right-sized for our stage, built to scale toward ISO 9001.
- **Set the safety foundation:** OSHA-compliant from day one, with safety built into the process, not bolted onto it.

### Run Phase (ongoing)
- **Throughput:** own output, lead time, and on-time delivery for all fabricated parts. Find the constraint, manage the constraint.
- **Financial performance:** own the fabrication P&L — cost per unit, scrap and rework, labor efficiency — and capex planning with payback analysis. Report in numbers the CFO and the floor can both act on.
- **Supply chain:** own metals sourcing — sheet, plate, tube, and hardware — including service-center and mill relationships, buffer strategy for long-lead materials, and supplier quality for what stays outsourced.
- **DFM partnership:** work daily with engineering to design parts for manufacturability and compress prototype-to-production cycles as the product evolves.

## What Success Looks Like (first 12 months)

- The fabrication line is commissioned and shipping production parts to schedule.
- The first targeted part families run in-house at or below the modeled cost per unit, verified against actuals — not estimates.
- Standard lead time on in-house fabricated parts is under one week.
- A trained, stable team runs the floor against metrics posted where everyone can see them.
- Within two quarters of each part transition, in-house quality escapes are at or below the outsourced baseline.
- Zero lost-time safety incidents.

- Hands-on command of the core equipment set: fiber laser cutting, press brakes, CNC machining (mills and lathes), turret punching, welding and weld fixturing, tube and roll bending, hardware insertion, and finishing (powder coat or equivalent). - Quality systems experience: building or running inspection regimes, root-cause and corrective action, and supplier quality.
- Startup temperament: comfortable with ambiguity, biased to action, hands-on enough for the floor, strategic enough for the boardroom.

**Nice to have:**

- Lean/Six Sigma background (we'll take TOC fluency over a belt color, but both is better).
- Experience scaling manufacturing in a venture-backed or high-growth company.
- ERP/MES implementation experience.
- Engineering or operations degree — or the equivalent earned on the floor.
