# Vice President of Fabrication

**Location:** Norcross, GA (on-site)
**Reports to:** Chief Operations Officer
**Type:** Full-time, Executive

---

## The Mission

We're bringing fabrication in-house — and we need a leader to build it from the ground up. You will stand up, staff, and scale a metals fabrication operation that becomes a competitive weapon: faster iteration loops for engineering, lower unit costs, tighter quality control, and supply chain resilience that outsourced fab can't match.

This is a builder's role. You'll make the equipment decisions, design the floor, hire the team, define the processes, and then run the department you created. You'll have real capital to deploy and real accountability for what it returns.

## How We Operate

We run at startup speed: we ship, measure, and iterate. Decisions that take a quarter elsewhere take a week here. We expect an aggressive drive for action — a strong plan executed today beats a perfect plan executed next month.

On the floor, we think in terms of Eli Goldratt's **Theory of Constraints**. You should be fluent in it — not as trivia, but as a daily operating system: identify the constraint, exploit it, subordinate everything else to it, elevate it, repeat. You measure the operation in throughput, inventory, and operating expense, and you know why a "cost savings" that starves the bottleneck is a loss. Drum-buffer-rope scheduling and throughput accounting should feel like home.

## What You'll Own

### Build Phase (first 6–12 months)
- **Stand up the fabrication department:** equipment selection and procurement, facility layout, installation, and commissioning — designed around flow, not departmental silos.
- **Make-vs-buy strategy:** model which parts and assemblies move in-house first, in what sequence, and prove out the economics of each transition.
- **Hire and develop the team:** recruit machine operators, fabricators, welders, and supervisors; build a training pipeline and a culture of ownership and safety.
- **Define the quality system:** first-article inspection, in-process checks, incoming material verification, and the documentation backbone to support it — right-sized for our stage, built to scale.

### Run Phase (ongoing)
- **Throughput:** own output, lead time, and on-time delivery for all fabricated parts. Find the constraint, manage the constraint.
- **Financial performance:** own cost-per-unit, scrap and rework rates, capex planning with payback analysis, and the fabrication P&L. Report in numbers the CFO and the floor can both act on.
- **Supply chain:** own metals sourcing — sheet, plate, tube, and hardware — including mill and service-center relationships, buffer strategy for long-lead materials, and supplier quality for what stays outsourced.
- **DFM partnership:** work daily with engineering to design parts for manufacturability, compress prototype-to-production cycles, and keep design iteration fast as products evolve.
- **Safety:** an uncompromised standard. Build it into the process, not onto it.

## What Success Looks Like (first 12 months)

- In-house fabrication line is commissioned and producing production parts.
- Targeted part families have transitioned in-house at or below the modeled cost-per-unit.
- Lead time on fabricated parts is measured in days, not weeks.
- A trained, stable team is running the floor with clear metrics visible to everyone on it.
- Quality escapes from in-house fab trend at or below the outsourced baseline within two quarters of each part transition.

## What You Bring

- **10+ years in metals fabrication and manufacturing**, with at least 5 years leading multi-discipline fabrication operations (director level or above).
- **Deep hands-on knowledge of the core equipment set:** laser cutting (fiber/CO₂), press brakes, CNC machining (mills and lathes), turret punching, welding and weld fixturing, tube and roll bending, hardware insertion, and finishing (powder coat or equivalent).
- **Experience standing up or significantly expanding a fabrication operation** — equipment justification, layout, installation, and ramp.
- **Working command of the Theory of Constraints** applied in a real production environment, with results you can point to.
- **High financial acumen:** capex modeling, make-vs-buy analysis, cost accounting (and its limits), budget ownership, and the ability to defend an investment case to executives and a board.
- **Supply chain depth:** metals sourcing and negotiation, supplier development, and inventory strategy that protects throughput without burying cash in stock.
- **Quality systems experience:** building or running inspection regimes, root-cause/corrective action, and supplier quality. ISO 9001 familiarity preferred.
- **Startup temperament:** comfortable with ambiguity, biased to action, hands-on enough to walk the floor and read a print, strategic enough to own a department-level P&L.

## Nice to Have

- Experience fabricating for robotics, automation equipment, or material handling products.
- Lean/Six Sigma background (we'll take TOC fluency over a belt color, but both is better).
- Experience scaling manufacturing in a venture-backed or high-growth company.
- ERP/MES implementation experience.

## Why This Role

You'll build a department, not babysit one. Your decisions on equipment, people, and process will be visible in the product and the P&L within months. You'll report directly to the COO with a seat at the table where company-level operations strategy gets made.
