Where does Supply Chain report?

Two needs, both real, both serving the same objective — pulling toward two org structures that cannot coexist. The diagram below is the argument; the audit below that is where it breaks.

OWNERJeff

READ ALOUD

IN ORDER TOmaximize revenue flow, parts must sync tightly with the build schedule — so SC must report to Production.

BUT IN ORDER TOmaximize revenue flow, SC must also serve field parts, cash, and strategy with independent judgment — so SC must stand alone.

It can’t do both.

Assumption audit

13 ASSUMPTIONS ACROSS 5 EDGES

A→B The objective requires the sync need
  1. 01because delivery date = revenue start
  2. 02because parts-into-build is today’s constraint
  3. 03because desync shows up directly as slipped go-lives
A→C The objective requires the whole-system need
  1. 01because revenue continues AFTER go-live (>70% of field tickets need parts)
  2. 02because growth must convert to margin and cash
  3. 03because supplier strategy outlives any single build
B→D The sync need requires reporting into Production
  1. 01because the ONLY way to synchronize two functions is common managementWEAKEST
  2. 02because what Production needs from SC = what the company needs from SC
  3. 03because Eric has the bandwidth and sourcing depth to run it well
C→D′ The whole-system need requires standing alone
  1. 01because ONLY structural independence protects non-factory priorities
  2. 02because an SC leader inside Production can’t refuse a margin-destroying expedite
  3. 03because Jeff has capacity for another strong direct report
D↔D′ The two wants exclude each other
  1. 01because SC must report to exactly one place, whole and undivided

WEAKEST ASSUMPTION

B→D #01

“Synchronization requires common management.”

This is the load-bearing link in the whole diagram: it is the only thing forcing the sync need onto a reporting line. Break it — show that parts and build can be synchronized by mechanism rather than by org chart — and the conflict evaporates without either need being sacrificed.
